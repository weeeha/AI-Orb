Orb Design Language

Design system foundation · Super-AI-Components

The orb, taken apart

Eleven AI-presence orbs, reviewed frame by frame — two of them by reading the shipped shader source. They turn out to be five species of one organism, assembled from the same eight layers.

Every orb here is live WebGL running the archetype it describes, and the one in Reaction listens to your microphone. Nothing on this page is a video.

Anatomy

The eight layers

Every orb in the corpus is a subset of this stack, and no orb uses all of it. Which layers you switch on is what produces a recognisable style. The order is also the render order, so it maps directly onto shader passes.

00GroundBackground, contact shadow, environment reflections
01MediumThe substance filling the body — fluid, noise field, or void
02ContentsDiscrete bodies inside — lobes, ribbons, petals
03CoreA single bright emissive point, sometimes with rays
04ShellRefractive boundary — fresnel rim, chromatic edge
05SpecularOne or two hard highlights that read as glass
06SurfaceGrain, striations, sparkle — detail at the finest scale
07MaskThe circular clip and how soft its edge is

Behaviour

Reaction

An orb that only loops is wallpaper. This one is wired to two independent signals the way a real voice agent is: output, the energy of the agent’s own speech, and input, the energy of yours. They must never look the same.

State · Idle

Agent speech — output signal

Synthesised in the browser with Web Audio and read back through an analyser — the orb is reacting to a real waveform, not a scripted animation.

Your voice — input signal

Nothing is recorded, transmitted, or stored — the level is read frame by frame and thrown away.

Finish

On Plasma, move your pointer across the globe — the filaments reach for it, the way they reach for a fingertip on real glass.

Output 0.00
Input 0.00

Output swells the body and accelerates the interior flow. Input never touches the body — it fires a ring travelling outward from the centre and lifts the rim. That asymmetry is the whole point: a person glancing at a still frame can tell who is talking.

Taxonomy

Six archetypes

Named by what they are made of, not by who made them. Four are safe defaults, one is a specialty, and one belongs on a launch page and nowhere else.

Ink

Adopt

One pigmented substance fills a rigid circle and churns. No shell, no contents, no specular — colour saturates the entire body and motion comes from domain-warped noise layered over a fluid field. It is the densest of the five, and the only one that survives being cropped.

Its weakness hides in plain sight: with no shell and no highlight, grain is carrying the realism almost single-handedly. Turn grain off and Ink collapses into a gradient.

Pearl

Adopt

A pale, near-opaque body carries a narrow saturated band across its equator, with a hard specular highlight and fine striations combed along the band. The most product-like of the five, and the one that most reliably reads as an object rather than an effect.

Pearl borrows the page background as its body colour, which is exactly why it dissolves on a dark theme. It does not get recoloured for dark mode — it gets replaced by Aurora.

Vessel

Adopt

A transparent shell of constant size holds discrete inner bodies that drift, merge and separate. The ratio of contents to shell is the signal: contents pressed against the glass read as full attention, contents shrunk to the centre read as idle.

The most legible archetype for state, because the shell gives the eye a fixed ruler to measure against. ElevenLabs’ own shipped component is structurally this — seven soft ovals composited in polar space.

Aurora

Adopt

The body goes transparent to a dark ground and colour survives only as a luminous wave, with a thin rim light doing the work of holding the silhouette together. Calm, nocturnal, and quiet enough to sit beside a paragraph someone is reading.

Aurora and Pearl are the same design in two value structures. That pairing is the single most useful thing in the corpus: dark mode is a light-model swap, not a palette swap.

Plasma

Specialty

A hard electrode at the centre throws branching filaments through an ionised haze to the inside of the glass. The only archetype whose reference is a physical object rather than a render, and the only one where the interior is made of lines instead of fields.

It earns its place on one behaviour nothing else offers. A real plasma globe bends its filaments toward whatever touches the glass, which gives input a literal, physical reading: the orb reaches for the person speaking. Output brightens the discharge and swells the core; input pulls the filaments outward until they bloom against the shell.

Marked specialty rather than default for honest reasons: filaments are the highest frequency detail in the whole taxonomy, so it aliases into noise below roughly 96 px, and its haze only works on a dark field.

Prism

Hero only

Dense combed strands of the full spectrum sweep across an emissive sphere bright enough to light its surroundings. Spectacular, and genuinely the right answer for a launch moment or a brand film.

It is excluded from the component on purpose. With every hue already at maximum saturation there is no headroom left to signal anything — the orb cannot get more attentive or more alarmed, because it is already at eleven.

Rules

Eleven principles

Each is falsifiable against the corpus — every rule below is a pattern that held across pieces by different studios who were not copying each other.

P1

Separate the vessel from the volatile

Something must stay constant for change to be readable. Give the orb a stable identity — silhouette, shell, rim — and let only the interior carry state.

P2

Motion belongs to the interior

The boundary should be the calmest thing in the composition. Hero renders deform their silhouettes freely because they own the whole frame; a component sits next to focus rings and text baselines. Budget ±2% radius, spend the rest inside.

P3

Saturated colour is a minority

In every reference that reads as premium rather than decorative, strong colour holds well under half the area — a band, a wave, three lobes, a rim. Colour everywhere is the signature of a screensaver.

P4

Flip the light model, not the palette

Light field: opaque pale body, tinted band, contact shadow. Dark field: transparent body, emissive band, rim light. Recolouring one into the other produces mud.

P5

One brightest point

Every convincing orb has exactly one luminance peak — a specular, a core burst, a caustic streak, a rim segment. It is what separates material from gradient. Two competing peaks read as a rendering error.

P6

Detail at the finest scale

Grain, striations, sparkle. Past taste, it is functional: it defeats gradient banding on 8-bit displays and gives the eye something to resolve at rest. Never ship a perfectly smooth orb.

P7

Breath, not activity

Ambient cycles in the corpus run 5–24 seconds; reactions run 0.8–1.5. Idle motion has to be slow enough to sit beside text someone is reading.

P8

State must survive a still frame

Pause any frame of the strongest references and you can tell roughly what the system is doing. If two states differ only in tempo, nobody can tell them apart in a screenshot — or at a glance, which is how orbs are actually seen.

P9

Never a centred radial gradient

A symmetrical glow centred in a circle is the universal signature of a loading spinner. Every reference breaks symmetry: bands sit off-equator, cores sit off-centre, waves lean. Asymmetry is what makes an orb read as present rather than pending.

P11

Detail is a function of size

Every reference here is a hero render, so none of them had to survive being 32 px in a chat transcript. Below roughly 96 px, grain and filaments stop reading as texture and start reading as dither. The orb has to shed its own finest scale as it shrinks.

P10

Hearing and speaking must not look alike

Give the two signals different anatomy, not different amplitudes of the same move. Output belongs to the body; input belongs to the boundary. When both are just “bigger”, the orb stops reporting who holds the turn.

Downstream

What this changes in the component

  1. 01Finish becomes a first-class prop — ink, pearl, vessel, aurora, plus plasma as a documented specialty. Prism is deliberately excluded.
  2. 02Dark mode is a light-model switch. Each preset declares both fields, and Pearl resolves to Aurora rather than being recoloured.
  3. 03Two audio signals, never one. outputLevel drives the body; inputLevel drives the boundary.
  4. 04Levels arrive as refs, not props, so a talking agent never triggers a React render.
  5. 05The silhouette stays rigid. The wobble that makes hero renders feel alive is not adopted.
  6. 06Nothing is fetched at runtime. Noise is generated in the shader; no textures, no CDN.
